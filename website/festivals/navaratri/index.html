Significance & Meaning

Navaratri (Sanskrit: नवरात्रि, Telugu: నవరాత్రి) literally means "nine nights" — nava (nine) + ratri (nights). It is one of the most widely celebrated Hindu festivals, observed four times a year, with Sharada Navaratri (autumn Navaratri) being the most prominent.

The festival celebrates the victory of divine feminine power (Shakti) over evil. Different traditions associate Navaratri with different themes: the defeat of the demon Mahishasura by Goddess Durga (Shakta tradition), the nine forms of Goddess Parvati, and the period of Lord Rama's worship of Goddess Durga before defeating Ravana.

In Telugu households, Navaratri is celebrated with Bommala Koluvu — the display of dolls and figurines arranged on stepped platforms (Golu), which is a distinctive cultural tradition of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

Puja Procedure

Daily Morning Puja (Pratah Puja)

  1. Sankalpa — Take a vow/intention for the day's worship, mentioning your name, gotra, and purpose
  2. Kalasha Sthapana — Establish the sacred Kalasha (pot) with water, mango leaves, and coconut (done on Day 1; maintained throughout)
  3. Devi Avahana — Invoke the Goddess with appropriate mantras for the day's form
  4. Shodashopachar Puja — Sixteen-step worship: Padya, Arghya, Achamana, Snana, Vastra, Gandha, Pushpa, Dhupa, Dipa, Naivedya, Tambula, Aarti, Pradakshina, Sashtanga Pranam, Pushpanjali, Visarjana
  5. Durga Saptashati Path — Reading of the 700 verses of Durga Saptashati (or relevant chapters)
  6. Aarti — Perform Aarti with camphor and ghee lamp
  7. Prasad distribution

Evening Puja (Sandhya Puja)

A shorter evening puja with lamp lighting (Deepa), aarti, and devotional singing (bhajans). In Telugu tradition, the lamp is kept lit throughout the nine nights (Akhanda Jyothi).

Fasting Rules — ఉపవాసం

Navaratri fasting is observed with varying levels of strictness based on individual tradition and capacity:

  • Full fast: Only water and milk; one meal of fruits at sunset
  • Partial fast: Avoid grains; consume sabudana (sago), fruits, milk, and specific fasting foods
  • Sattvic diet: Avoid non-vegetarian food, onion, garlic, alcohol throughout the nine days

Who should avoid strict fasting: Pregnant women, nursing mothers, children, elderly, and those with health conditions should not fast without medical consultation. Devotion is the essence, not physical austerity alone.

Naivedyam — నైవేద్యం (Daily Offerings)

Traditional naivedyam (food offerings) varies by day:

  • Day 1 (Shailaputri): Cow's ghee
  • Day 2 (Brahmacharini) — Today: Sugar and panchamrit
  • Day 3 (Chandraghanta): Milk and kheer
  • Day 4 (Kushmanda): Malpua
  • Day 5 (Skandamata): Banana
  • Day 6 (Katyayani): Honey
  • Day 7 (Kalaratri): Jaggery (gur)
  • Day 8 (Mahagauri): Coconut
  • Day 9 (Siddhidatri): Sesame (til)

Telugu Traditions — తెలుగు సంప్రదాయాలు

Bommala Koluvu (బొమ్మల కొలువు)

The most distinctive Navaratri tradition in Telugu households is Bommala Koluvu — the display of dolls, figurines, and religious images arranged on stepped platforms (usually 9 or 11 steps, representing the nine nights and auspicious odd numbers). The display typically includes:

  • Divine figures (deities, mythological characters)
  • Traditional crafts and artisanal figures
  • Natural scenes and village life dioramas
  • New dolls added each year as a family tradition

Sumangalis (married women) visit each other's homes to see the Koluvu, exchange gifts (tamboolam: betel leaves, fruits, kumkum), and enjoy traditional songs (Harikatha, devotional music).

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Navaratri 2026 start and end?
Sharada Navaratri 2026 begins on October 11, 2026 (Ashwin Shukla Pratipada) and the nine nights conclude on October 19, 2026 (Maha Navami). Vijayadasami (Dussehra) falls on October 20, 2026.
What is Bommala Koluvu?
Bommala Koluvu (literally "display of dolls") is a Telugu tradition during Navaratri where families arrange dolls, figurines, and religious images on stepped platforms. This tradition is specific to Andhra Pradesh and Telangana households and represents a living cultural heritage passed down through generations.
Can we eat non-vegetarian food during Navaratri?
Traditionally, Navaratri fasting involves abstaining from non-vegetarian food, onions, garlic, and alcohol. However, the level of observance is a personal and family choice. The core of Navaratri is devotion and spiritual practice.
Which Goddess is worshipped on Day 1 (October 11)?
Day 1 of Navaratri (October 11, 2026) begins with Ghatasthapana and is dedicated to Goddess Shailaputri — the daughter of the Himalayas. The mantra is: ॐ देवी शैलपुत्र्यै नमः।
How are Navaratri dates calculated?
Navaratri dates are determined by the Hindu Panchangam — specifically Ashwin Shukla Pratipada (the first day of the waxing moon in the month of Ashwin).
